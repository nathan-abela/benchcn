## Usage

```tsx
import { SignalStrength } from "@/components/ui/signal-strength";

<SignalStrength value={rssi} />
<SignalStrength unit="%" value={linkQuality} />
```

An ESP32 reports RSSI from `WiFi.RSSI()`. Send it as a sample on its own channel and pass `readings.rssi?.v`.

## Default thresholds

With `unit="dBm"` the bars follow Android's Wi-Fi levels: at or below -100 dBm shows none, at or above -55 dBm shows all, and the bars split the range between evenly. For four bars that is -88.75, -77.5, -66.25 and -55 dBm. The numbers come from `WifiManager.calculateSignalLevel` in the Android 10 source.

With `unit="%"` the defaults are plain even steps (20, 40, 60 and 80 for four bars), not taken from any standard.

These are Wi-Fi numbers. For cellular, LoRa or anything else, pass `thresholds` from your radio's documentation.

## Examples

### Custom bars

One threshold per bar, lowest first. The number of thresholds sets the number of bars.

```tsx
<SignalStrength value={rssi} thresholds={[-95, -85, -75]} />
```

### Bars only

```tsx
<SignalStrength value={rssi} showValue={false} />
```

## Theming

Lit bars use `--foreground` and unlit bars the same colour at 15 % opacity. A weak signal is not a fault, so the bars never change colour.

## Accessibility

A visually hidden native `meter` carries the bar count and a text such as "2 of 4 bars, -67 dBm". The reading is also shown as text unless `showValue` is off.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion` bars switch without fading.
